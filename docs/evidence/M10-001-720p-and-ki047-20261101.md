Harness rules at 720p: the GPU rule does not fire (<85%), the CP rule does not fire (CP
busy 82-87% < 90%, no fence wait in it), read-back is about 7-8% of CP busy, WAIT_REG_MEM
register waits zero; the render-thread rule fires (work above 90% of the interval, spin
share under 5%). The guest sampler agrees: the render thread is almost never in a wait
(`NtWaitForSingleObjectEx` about 1.2% of samples), with a flat profile (top function
`82422160` about 6% of samples, top 12 about 33%), `RtlEnter/LeaveCriticalSection` the
dominant last imports, and 1.1-1.8 ms/frame of kernel time (7-10% of its CPU).

**Headroom at 720p** (cap = 1000 / ms per frame, against the segment fps):

| Resource | Cap fps | Headroom |
|---|---|---|
| Render thread | it is the wall | 0-7% (only the spin share is slack) |
| CP, own busy time only | 65-76 | +16-22% |
| CP, busy + wrm_mem (if wrm_mem is a fixed per-frame cost) | 58-66 | **+3-6%** |
| GPU | 77-81 | +29-37% |

The wrm_mem term decides how close the CP really is. It is **1.93 ms per frame at 720p
and at 1440p alike** (38 and 60 fps, with and without the CP blocking on GPU fences),
which points to a fixed cost per frame rather than waiting on the render thread. Source
(l53 SDK `ExecutePacketType3_WAIT_REG_MEM`): when a memory wait is not satisfied on the
first poll and the packet's `wait >= 0x100`, the CP calls
`Sleep(milliseconds(wait / 0x100))` because `vsync` is true (the log shows `vsync true`),
so every such episode costs at least one OS sleep quantum, probably one or two episodes per
frame. Under that reading the CP is effectively co-limiting at 720p (3-6% from the wall),
with only the ring idle (0.3-0.9 ms/frame) as true slack.

**1440p comparison.** Per-frame CPU costs are 15-40% higher at 1440p for the same
content: CP 1.22-1.90 against 1.09-1.27 us/draw (every phase, not only `p_tex`), render
work 16.6-23.0 against 15.5-16.5 ms/frame. A uniform slowdown on two threads while the GPU
is at 99% fits laptop CPU/GPU power sharing (lower CPU clocks) or wake-up costs after
fence waits; not provable from these lines (the 1.99 GHz TSC is not the core clock). So
the 1440p "second wall" estimates (render ~49 fps, CP ~52 fps if the GPU got faster) are
pessimistic. A two-point fit of GPU time (12.4 ms at 1x pixels, 25.8 ms at 4x) gives
roughly **7.9 ms/frame of resolution-independent GPU time + 4.5 ms per 720p-worth of
pixels**, with 2,000-3,200 barriers and 32-41 k replayed commands per frame.

**Replay worker gap: repeats and grows.** Worker CPU is 70-75% of a core while the timed
replay is only 3.0-3.6 ms/frame, so 8.2-9.6 ms/frame (50-55% of a core) is outside the
timed replay, against 4.8-7.8 ms/frame (18-30% of a core) at 1440p. It scales with frame
and submission rate (4.4-4.6 submissions/frame, 5.5-7.5 worker sleeps per frame): a
spin-wait between streamed chunks is the likely cause. Separately, the timed replay itself
is 9-10 ms/frame at 1440p but 3.0-3.6 at 720p for the same command counts, so at 1440p it
includes GPU back-pressure. It is not a limiter on this PC, but it burns a core that
shares the laptop's power budget (and would matter more on a 4-core Deck).

**M10-006 (multi-threaded CP): not justified as the next step.** The CP is not the first
wall at either resolution (render thread at 720p, GPU at 1440p). It is a close second at
720p, so any render-thread gain beyond about 5% will hit the CP. But cheaper
single-threaded CP cuts come first and together exceed that margin: the wrm_mem sleep
(1.93 ms/frame, about 12% of the 720p frame), `p_tex` + `p_bind` (3.7-4.6 ms/frame),
upload (2.0-2.5 ms/frame) and watch arm/disarm (0.8-1.0 ms/frame). Keep M10-006 as a
gated fallback: reconsider only if, after render-thread work and these CP cuts, CP busy +
wrm_mem stays at or above about 95% of wall at 720p, or a Deck arm shows the CP first
there (no Deck data in this set).

**Next M10 targets (in order):**

1. **Render thread (720p wall).** Attribute its time: write-watch faults per thread (total

### 4.4 Re-ranked causes

1. **Stale `some` read-back of a re-used composite key (H2): rank 1, medium-high.**
   With `readback_resolve=some`, a resolve to a still-live key (same address and length
   used within 60 frames) is not a miss: 3 of 4 such resolves copy nothing (guest memory
   keeps what it had) and the 4th copies the previous same-key slot. If every car's
   livery composite resolves to one scratch address, a police car whose composite comes
   within about 1-1.6 s of another car's composite gets that other composite (or an older
   one) baked into its atlas for its life. This is **staleness by design, decided by the
   traffic pattern and the copy counter's phase**, not by timing: independent of the
   shader store, of resolution scale and of CP/GPU load, rare (needs two composites close
   together, so dependent on traffic density and time spent driving), with history-
   dependent content (different looks per occurrence; blue only once) and intact decals
   if they are separate textures. It fits every observation, including the warm 720p
   pair. It predicts that `readback_resolve=full` sessions never break and that the diag v2
   `resolve2` line for the livery key shows `miss=0` with `decision=deferred` or `read=prev`.
2. **Previous-slot read whose GPU copy was not finished, or a new NotZeroed slot (timing
   sub-case of 1): medium-low.** Still possible, but at 720p the GPU had slack
   (readback_wait 0.04 ms/frame, fence about 0), which narrows the in-flight window; two
   catches in the least GPU-loaded configuration argue for 1 over this.
3. **Texture-cache staleness of the atlas (upload racing the guest CPU writer, or a missed
   invalidation): medium-low.** Independent of store and scale, so still compatible; no
   signal (0 protect failures, normal gap-lock misses and already-cleared counts). Diag v2
   `tex_load`/`tex_inval` lines cover it.
4. **CP lag against an unsynchronised guest read: low** (was rank 1 in section 8.3 of the
   1440p document). Its only store/scale-dependent driver (cold pipeline waits, GPU-bound
   fence stalls) is absent in Run B, and the CP was not behind.
5. **EDRAM aliasing / ownership transfer: scale-specific variants excluded** (scale 1);
   a deterministic scale-1 aliasing would break far more often. Low.
6. **Excluded:** H1 pending-pipeline skip (0 skips in every l53 session, three
   occurrences), H3 shifted scaled copy (scale 1 here; 0 in l50), VRAM pressure
   (2.6 of 7.1 GB), LOD/mip blur, genuine livery.

Answer to the question: yes. The observation set is now most consistent with a
**staleness mechanism independent of cache state and scale**, whose odds grow with time
spent driving in traffic (density of livery composites), and the leading candidate is the
`some` read-back's non-copy/previous-slot path rather than a pure timing race.

### 6.3 The livery composite key and its `some` handling (evidence)

The composite pass is visible through the `wait_resolve` lines (logged because a new
pipeline had to be waited for right before the resolve) and the `overlap` lines:

Reading (frame = guest frame; the catch F10 is at guest frame about 6938):

- At the very start of a frame (draws 3-17), the game renders at EDRAM base 0 a **1024x1024
  32bpp image and resolves it to the scratch range 050A0000 (4 MB), then a full mip chain
  512x512 -> 8x8 into the same scratch range** (the 512x512 level at the same base address
  050A0000, the smaller levels after it). This is the shape of the livery/vinyl composite
  that the CPU then turns into the car's atlas (KI-033: 1024x1024 DXT5): 1024x1024 RGBA8
  plus mips, square power-of-two, start-of-frame, shared scratch. It is the first direct
  sighting of the composite's resolve keys.
- **The scratch range is a shared key in the sense of candidate 1**: two keys of different
  length start at the same address (050A0000/4 MB for level 0, 050A0000/1 MB for level 1),
  and every composite re-uses them. Under `some`, a resolve to a key still alive (used within
  60 frames) is not a miss and is copied only every 4th time.
- **Two composite resolves are shown taking exactly the stale path**: frame 826 (level 0,
  1024x1024, during the world load when the player's and traffic cars are composited back to
  back) and **frame 5946, 15:57:15.8, 25.5 s before the catch, during lap 1 while the police
  encounter was building up**: level 1 (512x512) at 050A0000, `decision=deferred read=prev`,
  i.e. **nothing was copied to guest memory for this composite**; the guest then reads
  whatever the range last held. Because level 0 and level 1 start at the same address, that
  can be the first megabyte of another car's (or this car's) tiled 1024x1024 level 0 read as
  a tiled 512x512 image, which gives regularly column-structured content, or an older mip of
  another car. The frame-5946 composite needed a new pipeline on this cold store (3.3 ms
  wait at draw 4), so it used a shader variant not composited before in the session, as a new
  livery type (police) would.
- Other in-window signals: CP stalls from pipeline waits were present (frames 6775-6806,
  15:57:37-38: 42, 22, 10 and 5 ms; `cp_wait5_us` up to 93 ms), but the same defect appears
  in warm sessions with none (section 4), so CP stalls are not required. The routine
  `overlap` lines (256x256 fmt 6 resolves inside the 640x640 depth key 06E79000; 224x224 at
  06ACD000 inside the 1280x512 scene key) recur all session long, broken or clean, and are
  not livery-specific.

**What the evidence supports:** candidate 1, in its shared-scratch form: **the livery
composite and its mips are resolved into one re-used scratch range with keys of different
length, and `readback_resolve=some` demonstrably defers (does not copy) composite resolves
there**, including one 25 s before the catch in the same lap. That is a direct mechanism by
which a car's atlas is built from stale or differently-sized content, independent of the
store and of the resolution scale. **What it does not prove:** that the frame-5946 composite
was the broken police car's (the car is not in the frame, and the per-key `resolve2` lines
for the composite range in frames 6406-6937 were cut by the budget). Candidate 2 (texture
upload racing the CPU writer) is not tested by this session (its lines were cut too);
candidate 3 (CP stall) is not required; scale-specific EDRAM paths are excluded by the 720p
catches.

### 6.4 Consequence for the cause ranking

Candidate 1 moves to **high** (mechanism observed on the composite key itself; fits all six
broken observations), candidate 2 stays medium-low (untested), candidate 3 low, scale-specific
EDRAM excluded. A cheap confirmation is also possible without more logging: if the defect is
a stale lower mip, a broken car should look **worse at a distance than up close** (the owner
could note this on the next catch).

### 6.5 Proposed diag v3 changes (small) and a fix A/B

1. **Composite-range rule for `resolve2`** (separate budget, e.g. 512 lines per minute; at
   car spawns only): log every read-back resolve with `src_base=0`, square power-of-two size
   8x8-2048x2048 and fmt 6/32bpp in the first 64 draws of a frame, **or** whose destination
   lies in a range already resolved with a different length (learned from `overlap`), with
   the existing fields plus the other key's last decision and copy frame.
2. **Raise the sporadic threshold** from 2 to at least 8 frames (removes the 128x128
   every-other-frame tiles: about 16 lines per frame now).
3. **`tex_load`: drop `rb_overlap` as a trigger** (true for 99.98% of loads); log
   `atlas_shape=1` loads (1-24 per minute) and loads overlapping the composite ranges of rule
   1, with the last resolve decision of every key overlapping the load (not only one).
   `tex_inval` only for logged loads.
4. **Pace budgets** with a per-second token bucket (for example 8 lines per second, burst 64)
   instead of front-loading per minute, and add an **F10 flight recorder**: keep the last
   4096 candidate lines of each kind in a ring buffer and write them to the log (and the
   package) on F10. The owner presses F10 within seconds of seeing the car, so the composite
   at the car's spawn is then always in the buffer.
5. **Fix A/B, decisive and cheap:** an option that always copies (fenced, `read=this`) any
   read-back resolve in a shared-scratch range or matching rule 1, leaving `some` for the
   per-frame keys. Cost is a handful of extra copies per car spawn. At the current rate
   (broken in 3 of the last 5 sessions with lap driving: l53 1440p cold, l53 720p warm, l54
   1440p cold) a few clean sessions with the option on, against continued breakage with it
   off, would be strong evidence; `--readback_resolve=full` remains the blunt alternative
   (about 20 fps at 1440p).
