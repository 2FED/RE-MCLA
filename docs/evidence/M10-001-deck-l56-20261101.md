## 3. Deck verdict

**First wall: the CP thread (with read-back sync waits inside it), on a CPU that is
oversubscribed.** Not the GPU.

- GPU 38% (driving) / 54% (title) of wall: GPU rule does not fire.
- CP rule fires (borderline): CP busy 88-90% (driving windows 90.0, 91.0, 89.6%), GPU
  under 85%, render thread in its serial wait 10.5% (driving) / 25% (title) of wall.
  The CP is occupied 99.5-99.7% of wall (busy + wrm_mem), ring idle only 0.3-0.5%: it
  never runs dry, the render thread waits on it.
- Read-back sub-rule fires: copy + read-back = 14.2 of 73.1 ms (19.4% of CP busy;
  21.9% at title), almost all of it the forced composite sync wait.
- Render thread is a close second in driving: outside its serial wait 89.5% of wall,
  but on-CPU work only 67.6%; it is off-CPU 10-22% of wall outside the wait
  (preemption or contended locks: `RtlEnter/LeaveCriticalSection`, `KeLeaveCriticalRegion`,
  `KfReleaseSpinLock` dominate its last imports). AddRef `sub_82422160` is 10-19% of its
  samples (PC 6.2%).
- CPU oversubscription: the top 8 threads use 4.2-4.5 logical CPUs in driving on the
  4-core/8-thread APU (title 3.1). The CP is busy-minus-waits 73.7% of wall but on-CPU
  only 64.7%: about 9% of wall it should be working but is not running. Kernel time is
  large: CP 20.5% of wall, render 11-13%, and `XThread055C (F800068C)` 43-45%.
- New hot threads in driving (absent at title):
  - `XThread055C (F800068C)`: 82-84% of a core, **43-45% kernel**; samples 70%
    `82460270` with last import `KeDelayExecutionThread` (plus `XMsgInProcessCall` 15-18%):
    a guest polling/sleep loop that is cheap on Windows but costs a core under Wine
    (title: 10-12%).
  - `XThread0538 (F8000534)`: 75-81% of a core (PC equivalent 32-35%); imports
    `XAudioGetVoiceCategoryVolumeChangeMask`, `KeWaitForSingleObject`, `KfLowerIrql`:
    likely the guest audio/sound engine worker.
  - Replay worker 58% of a core with timed replay 21% (about 0.37 core outside replay,
    the known inter-chunk spin).
- Wait/pacing: stock 30 with vsync quantizes frames to 16.7 ms multiples (driving p50
  83 = 5 vblanks, p90 100 = 6); wrm_mem 7.6 ms/frame is mostly that quantization (the
  CP waiting for the vblank-gated present release), not the 1 ms sleep quantum.

**Compared with the PC 720p arm**: the PC is render-thread-first (93-98%) with the CP
close behind at 82-87% and the GPU at 74-78%. On the Deck the order flips: CP first
(occupied about 100%), render thread second (89% outside the wait, partly off-CPU), GPU
far behind (38-54%). Per-draw CP cost is 3.3x the PC overall (2.7-3.2x for the D3D12-ish
phases bind/tex/rt/pso/record, which is roughly the CPU speed ratio; 6-7x for prim and vb,
which contain shared-memory `RequestRange` and the watch lock), uploads are about 9x
per call, and the CP carries a 10.6 ms/frame forced read-back wait that the l53 PC arm
did not have.

## 4. Consequences for M10 (ranked for the Deck, ms per driving frame of 81.1)

1. **Memory-watch / upload path under Wine (M10-CPU-E, plus protect-call coalescing):**
   upload 23.4 ms (28.8% of wall), of which watch arm 14.7; disarm 5.9; write faults 8.7
   (all threads). 12.9 us per `VirtualProtect` arm call and 0.5 s/min of lock wait.
   The biggest Deck-specific CP item; on PC it is under 1 ms. Fewer protect calls (range
   coalescing, unprotected hot blocks with exact uploads) is the top Deck lever.
2. **KI-047 read-back sync (`MCLA_READBACK_SYNC`)**: 10.6 ms/frame fence wait on the CP
   in driving (13.1% of wall), 9.4 ms at title (16.3%), one or two full GPU drains per
   frame. If the CP stays the wall, removing most of it is worth about +12-15% fps
   (driving about 12.3 -> 14 fps before vsync quantization). Make it targeted: wait on
   the fence of the submission that holds the composite copy, or at the guest's CPU
   read, not `AwaitAllQueueOperationsCompletion` on every match.
3. **Free cores** (the CPU is oversubscribed; the CP loses about 9% of wall to not
   running): l57 replay batched wake (about 0.3-0.4 core), the `F800068C`
   `KeDelayExecutionThread` loop (about 0.8 core, half of it kernel), and check the
   audio-side worker (0.8 core).
4. **CP draw phases** (24.2 ms): bind + tex 2.05 us/draw = 12 ms; prim + vb 0.96 us/draw =
   5.7 ms (watch-lock/RequestRange costs, ties to item 1).
5. **l57 native refcount**: render-thread gain larger than on PC (AddRef 10-19% of its
   samples vs 6.2%) and it reduces global-mutex contention with the CP; matters once the
   CP is cut (render thread is second, ~10% behind).
6. **l57 WRM event wake**: small on the Deck in stock mode; wrm_mem 7.6 ms/frame is mostly
   vblank quantization; expect at most the sleep-quantum part (~1 ms).
7. **l58 read-back elision**: readback_memcpy 1.43 + readback_gpu 0.07 ms/frame; eliding
   about 74% of the bytes saves about 1 ms/frame (~1.3%). It does not touch the forced
   sync (forced counts are an invariant across its arms).
8. **M10-006 multi-threaded CP**: the gate in the 720p document ("or a Deck arm shows
   the CP first there") is **met**: the CP is the first wall on the Deck. But with
   4.2-4.5 busy logical CPUs on 4 cores there is no spare core for CP workers yet; do
   items 1-3 first (they remove about 30-40 ms of CP time per frame and free about one
   core), then re-measure. M10-006 moves from "not justified" to "justified on the Deck,
   after the Wine-specific single-thread cuts".
9. **Vulkan (M10-004 / l59)**: the vkd3d translation cost is not visible on the CP-side
   D3D12 calls (submit + execute + present 0.58 ms/frame; D3D12-ish phases about 3x PC,
   in line with the CPU ratio). It shows most in the replay worker (timed replay
   17.0 ms/frame, 5x PC for similar command counts, and 0.58 core), and possibly in
   p_bind (descriptor emulation). Fence waits are the read-back sync (backend-agnostic
   semantics) and GPU busy is not the wall. Expected effect of Vulkan: fewer CPU cycles in
   the replay worker (helps the oversubscribed CPU) and maybe bind; it does not remove
   the watch/upload, read-back sync or wrm costs. Moderate, not decisive; the l59 A/B
   decides (compare by guest outputs per second, KI-059).

## 7. Other findings and KI candidates

- **Audio underruns at driving start (catalogue candidate, KI-060 or a KI-027 note)**:
  F10 audit 3 (20:34:09-20:34:36, the first 27 s of driving after the load)
  `audio_underruns=2259` in 289 of 300 samples, `stream_stalls=2`, 2,268 stream reads
  (75 MB). Audits 1-2 and the last 84 samples before exit: 0. Fits audio tearing at the
  start of driving while the CPU is oversubscribed (audio-side worker 0.8 core, polling
  thread 0.8 core).
- **Deck performance profile (KI-027 update or new KI)**: l56 stock 30 under vkd3d:
  title 17.4 fps, driving 11.8-12.7 fps (owner: 10-14), p99 133 ms, 68-71 frames over
  100 ms per minute. CP-first, CPU-oversubscribed. For context only (not comparable
  builds/routes/back-ends): M7-era Deck runs logged about 18-19 outputs/s unpaused.
- **Guest polling thread `F800068C` under Wine**: 82-84% of a core, 43-45% kernel, in a
  `KeDelayExecutionThread` loop with `XMsgInProcessCall`, only in gameplay. Candidate
  host fix: make short/zero `KeDelayExecutionThread` intervals cheaper (or a real sleep)
  under Wine; verify against the PC first.
- **Watch/protect cost under Wine** (12.9 us per arm call, about 9x the PC per upload
  call): belongs with M10-CPU-E; worth a KI only if it is tracked as a Deck-specific
  issue.
- Warnings/errors: only known startup noise (VFS escape probes, read-only probes, 7

- Owner protocol note: the second "title idle" F10 (20:33:20) was not on the uniform
