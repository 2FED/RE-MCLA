# l79 Steam Deck A/B: default / timer / heaps64 / all3 (2026-11-08)

Read-only analysis by an Opus subagent on 2026-11-08 of the owner l79 Deck sessions (default / timer / heaps64 / all3; owner-copied archive treated as untrusted); accepted by the primary agent.

This is a read-only analysis of the four owner Deck sessions. All four use build 6609C738 (the l79 owner candidate), Proton, RADV VANGOGH, stock timing, 1280x720 at 1x, vsync on, and the same "usual city loop". Every session ended `complete exit 0`, and each has three F10 packages. Sources were the session `mcla.log` files (minute lines and F10 windows) and the F10 `state.json` / `manifest.json` files. Nothing was built, launched or executed. Save-private data, before/after-user data and minidumps were not opened. Spark was not used: these are deterministic parses of four logs, and checking a model's answer would cost more than doing the parse.

**Method.** Minute 1 of each session (title, boot load, menus, first launch) is excluded. "Driving" means pacing minutes 2..N:
- 5 minutes for default, 4 for timer, 3 for heaps64 and 3 for all3;
- the default arm's minute 5 includes an 18 s pause/save menu.

`MCLA_PERF_SPLIT` and `MCLA_THREAD_CPU` restart at each F10, so the F10 window is added to the following minute window. `MCLA_READBACK` / `READBACK_SYNC` minute lines already cover the whole minute. Per-frame values are window sums divided by frames.

**heaps64 outlier minute.** Minute 3 (13:26:32-13:27:32) was a light scene: 3176 draws per frame against about 6000 elsewhere, 23.0 fps, and no menu. "heaps64 (excl.)" drops that minute.

### Fences, read-back and CP lead (l78 defaults on the Deck)

- **No fence stalls in any arm.**
  - `fence_late_gt50ms=0` everywhere, and `fence_late_20_50ms` is 0 apart from 4 in the default arm's load minute.
  - `fence_late_f2p=0` and `fence_deferred_max_frames=1`; `fence_deferred_ms_max` is 10-17 ms (31-41 ms in two default minutes).
  - The fence queue maximum is 4-8, and `fence_immediate` is 0-14 per driving minute.
- **CP lead:** `cp_lead_frames` max 1 in every minute (the CP never runs more than one frame ahead); `cp_lead_subs` averages 1.01-1.3, max 3.
- **Steady exemption:**
  - It is active: one exempt key per frame (`steady_exempt` = frames), with `fallbacks_to_full=0`, `forced_wait_failed=0` and `guard_misses=0`.
  - Forced sync waits range from 16 to 874 per minute. Read-back wait follows them: 0.03 ms/f in quiet minutes, up to 2.4 ms/f in the minute with 874 forced waits.
- **Against l66.** "Forced SYNC wait" was 6.1-6.5 ms/f in l66 and fence wait is now 0.65-0.74 ms/f. Copy fell from 9.7 to 4.1 ms/f. Both savings were re-spent elsewhere (section 2).

**Verdict.**
- **No measurable Deck improvement from l78 defaults plus the l79 switches.**
  - fps is flat at about 15 at the same draw load.
  - The CP cost per draw is unchanged at about 10 us.
  - The 5-6 ms/f saved on forced sync waits and copies was re-spent: watch arm +3 ms/f, upload +2, packets_other about 11, and wrm_mem waiting on the guest.
  - Vsync quantisation keeps the median at 4 vblanks (66.7 ms). Reaching 3 vblanks (20 fps) needs a CP chain under about 50 ms, which is about -14 ms/f of CP work.
  - The owner's "slightly better" is within run-to-run and scene variance. The light heaps64 minute (23 fps at 3176 draws/f) shows that fps tracks draw load: CP busy is about 5.5 ms + 8.4 us per draw.
- **Where the time goes:**
  1. The CP thread: 57-60 ms/f busy. About 22 ms/f of it is memory-watch arm/disarm/write faults, which are Wine-expensive (kernel 11-12 ms/f).
  2. The guest render thread: about 46 ms/f of work, pinned at about 94 % of a core. It is the next wall once the CP drops below about 46 ms.
  3. The GPU: 28-29 ms/f, so the GPU alone could reach about 35 fps.

## 3. Audio crackle (KI-060)

- **The underruns follow the polling thread's high phase, not the arm.**
  - In every arm, the low-underrun audit (after F10 #1: 0-494) falls in windows where the guest polling thread F800068C runs at 41-47 %.
  - The high-underrun audit (after F10 #2: 1492-1982, 87-96 % of samples) falls in windows where that thread has risen to 66-84 % of a core, with 36-45 % kernel time.
  - In the high phase the audio-side guest thread F8000534 rises to 79-82 %, and the process reaches 4.9-5.0 cores. The render and CP threads stay pinned, so on 4C/8T every busy thread now shares a physical core with another.
- **Underruns coincide with long frames.** Inside the high audits, frames that carry an underrun have a 72-75 ms interval against 60-64 ms for clean frames (default, timer, heaps64; all3 74.7 vs 71.2).
- **Which thread starves the audio worker.** The likely cause is CPU contention on its SMT sibling and the scheduler: the polling thread's sleep/IPC loop (KeDelayExecutionThread + XMsgInProcessCall) at about 0.8 core, with nearly half of it in the Wine kernel. The audio worker's own demand also rises to about 0.8 core.
- **The timer switch did not change underruns.** Timer 1755 vs default 1492; all3 1982 vs heaps64 1717. It freed only about 0.02-0.03 core. XMA Canary fixes: zero triggers, no effect.
- **Unchanged since l64.** l64 "all" had 1467 per audit; l79 has 1492-1982 per audit.

- **Content errors:** the only non-zero content result in any session is the usual empty-enumerator 0x12 at boot. KI-068 is still fine on l79.

## 5. Deck recommendations

**l79 switches on the Deck**

- **`d3d12_texture_placed_heaps` (heaps64): no benefit on the Deck, so keep it off for Proton (or neutral).**
  - Under vkd3d-proton the committed path already costs 14-20 us per texture; on PC (NVIDIA) it was 400 us.
  - With heaps the average is 25 us. Heap creation adds 9.5-15.6 ms hitches (5 heaps during load, 2 more while driving at 64 MB).
  - p_tex is the same with or without heaps: 4.9-5.3 ms/f.
  - It is safe: 0 fallbacks, 0 failures, 448 MB reserved against 384 MB used.
  - If one default for all platforms is wanted, pre-create the heaps during loading.
- **`timer_queue_blocking_wait`: keep it on.** It works under Proton, the tick is more accurate (late >1 ms 8-11 % -> 1.2-2.4 %), and it showed no stall or lost wake. Expect no fps or audio gain. A cheap follow-up is `timer_queue_spin_tail_us` 50 (or 0) on the Deck: TimerQueue about 13 % -> about 5 % of a core; watch late_1ms.
- **`xma_canary_fixes`:** neutral on the Deck (zero triggers). Leave the decision to the PC by-ear result.

**Next Deck levers, ranked by expected effect**

1. **Memory-watch cost under Wine** (M10 hot blocks with batched copies, or coalesced protect calls).
   - This is the largest CP item: 22 ms/f (arm 11.0 + disarm 4.4 + write faults 6.6), about 39 % of CP busy, with 11-12 ms/f of CP kernel time.
   - The l66 hot-on run removed 5.9 ms/f but re-spent it on about 2000 copies between draws (GPU +10 ms/f).
   - Batching the copies per submission keeps that saving. Halving the watch cost (-11 ms/f) brings the CP chain to about 53 ms, which gives some 3-vblank frames: expected 15 -> 16.5-17.5 fps.
   - It also cuts the render thread's write faults, which is the second wall.
2. **CP draw/packet fast paths.**
   - Targets: packets_other 10.8 ms/f and draw 22.7 ms/f (p_bind 6.1, p_tex 5.1).
   - Fetch identity verify: 63 % of fetch dwords are rewritten unchanged, and texture memo hits are 72 %.
   - Bulk register writes would cut packets_other.
   - Realistic target -4 to -8 ms/f. Together with lever 1 this is what is needed to reach 3 vblanks (20 fps).
3. **M11-003 free Deck cores / KI-060 audio.** This is the crackle fix, not an fps fix.
   - Make the guest polling loop cheap under Wine. F800068C is 0.7-0.84 core in its high phase, about 45 % of it kernel: treat short or zero `KeDelayExecutionThread` intervals as a waitable-timer sleep or a coalesced 1 ms sleep, and look at the XMsgInProcessCall path.
   - Raise the audio worker F8000534 (the XAudio client path) to above-normal priority. Optionally keep it off the render/CP SMT siblings.
   - Expected: underruns from 1500-2000 per audit to near 0. The freed 0.4-0.8 logical CPU should also ease SMT contention on the CP and render threads (a small fps gain).
   - Measurable through the same post-F10 audit plus the F800068C share.
4. **Render thread (guest code, M10 hot blocks / guest-side costs).** It is pinned at about 94 %, with about 46 ms/f of work. It becomes the limit as soon as the CP drops below about 46 ms/f, and caps the Deck at about 21 fps until it is addressed.
5. **Small levers (do not prioritise):**
   - Threaded submission: replay is 24 % of a core, and submit + execute + present costs 0.86 ms/f.
   - `d3d12_fence_defer_min_gap_us`: fence_early submissions are only 1.1-1.3 per frame, so it saves under 0.1 ms/f. Fences are already stall-free.
   - A larger kick interval (draw_full is 8 of the 14 submissions per frame, but CP cost under 1 ms/f).
6. **Lower render scale: no effect.** The GPU is 42-46 % busy at 28.7 ms/f, and the Deck is CPU-bound. It is only relevant after levers 1, 2 and 4 bring the frame under about 30 ms.
