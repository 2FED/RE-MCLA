# M11 KI-082 clip A/B: vb-old (KI-088 fix off) vs clear (l120 defaults) (2026-11-22)

Read-only analysis by an Opus subagent. No builds, tests, game or launcher launches, no repo edits.
Only short-lived ffmpeg (`-threads 2`) and owner-Python processes, standard library only (numpy and
PIL are not installed). Spark not used: two videos measured with deterministic ffmpeg and Python
scripts. There was no text packet worth offloading, and checking a model's answer would have cost more
than the measurement.

Question: KI-082 (nearby moving traffic cars "tremble", mostly vertically; standing cars do not).
Does turning KI-088 (stale vertex data for buffers rewritten in place; live on slot 95) off or on change
the tremble? The owner's impression of this A/B: "as far as I can tell nothing changed".

Method and limits follow `M11-ki082-clip-analysis-20261117.md`. There is one change: car and world
positions come from luma template matching (SAD, coarse-to-fine, parabolic sub-pixel, keyframe
template refreshed every 4 unique frames) instead of thresholded light centroids.
- Why: in daylight, centroids of brake lights flicker by 5-10 px because of glow and threshold effects.
  Templates on body edges are stable to 0.1-0.4 px on world features.
- Units: all pixel values are 3840x2160 (4K) output pixels; 1 render pixel = 2 output pixels.

## 4. Rigid body motion or deformation (A4 taxi, B4 white car)

Correlation of the vertical residuals (k axis) between parts of the same car:

| pair | A4 taxi (vb-old) | B4 white car (clear) |
|---|---|---|
| light row vs bumper (non-overlapping regions) | **0.99** | **0.99** |
| rear-window top vs light row | 0.94 (amplitude 0.54 vs 1.67 px) | -0.65 (amplitude 2.23 vs 1.83 px) |
| rear wheel vs light row | -0.11 | 0.35 |
| shadow under the car vs light row | 0.06 | 0.19 |
| world references vs car | -0.2 .. 0.07 | -0.13 .. 0.18 |

- **The lower rear body moves as one rigid piece**, frame to frame, at 1.7-1.85 px RMS. This is not
  edge noise: two disjoint templates agree at r = 0.99.
- **The upper body moves differently**: smaller and in phase on the taxi; anti-phase on the white car.
  That points to a pitch or scale (depth) component in the body's per-frame pose jitter, not to pure
  heave. The glass and reflections in the rear-window template add noise.
- **Wheels and shadow do not share the body's motion** (|r| <= 0.35). The body jitters relative to its
  own wheels and shadow, which follow the road.
  - Caveat: the wheel and shadow trackers are noisier (dark, low-texture templates; 1.4-2.6 px).
- **Mean absolute difference maps after stabilising on the light row** (A4: 92 frame pairs;
  B4: 161 pairs):
  - The residual change is concentrated on the tail-light (brake-light intensity changes) and on thin
    horizontal edges (trunk line, plate outline): residual misregistration and edge crawl.
  - Flat areas: about 1.0-1.4 grey levels. Edge pixels: about 2.1-2.2.
  - Rows above the trunk (rear window and the background behind the car) change most; the bumper and
    shadow rows change least.
  - No ghost, doubled edge, or sub-part offset by one frame of motion in either clip.
  - The maps look the same in both arms.

KI-088 on screen: no stale-frame geometry artefact was found in the vb-old clip. That fits the counters:
in A's window the vb-old run had at most 26 (late window) or about 70 (early window) stale in-sync
skips, against about 3,000 frames and millions of buffer binds. Each stale skip affects one draw for one
frame (the two slot-95 buffers seen are 80 and 23,872 bytes). A one-frame glitch on one draw cannot
produce a continuous tremble on every nearby moving car, and nothing of the kind was seen in the sampled
crops.

## 5. Verdict

| hypothesis | prediction | measured | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| KI-088 stale vertex data causes KI-082 | tremble reduced or gone with the fix (clip B); part-lag or ghost artefacts in A | same car vertical term in both arms (A4 1.70 vs B4 1.84 px); 0-70 stale draws possible in A's window; no ghosts | **rejected** (high confidence, about 90 %) |
| presentation-timing judder | residual tied to capture or world-time intervals; affects world and player equally | vertical car term uncorrelated with frame intervals (r <= 0.2); world and player at 0.2-0.5 px | **not the vertical term** (it still explains the along-motion judder from the earlier analysis) |
| edge crawl / aliasing | change on edges only, uncorrelated between parts | coherent rigid motion (r = 0.99) between disjoint body parts | **minor** contributor |
| shadow flicker / LOD switch | change in the shadow rows or sudden steps | shadow rows change least; the body residual is white noise (ac1 at the noise baseline), not intermittent steps (a step count was not done) | **not supported** |
| traffic body pose (chassis/suspension) jitter per simulation step | rigid per-frame vertical (and pitch) jitter of the body relative to wheels, shadow and world; only when moving; larger when close | exactly this: 1.0-2.5 px RMS when moving, 0.4 px when standing; wheels and shadow uncorrelated; white noise per frame | **supported** (moderate-high, about 70 %) |

- The fix does not change the tremble: about 1.7-1.8 px RMS per frame for a close car slowing at the
  Broadway intersection in both arms, B/A = 1.08 (+-10 % per segment, +-50 % between segments).
- What the tremble most likely is: the traffic car's body pose jitters by about 0.5-1.2 render px per
  frame, mostly vertically and with a pitch component. The jitter is random from frame to frame,
  present only while the car moves, and not shared by its wheels or shadow. That is the signature of
  per-step noise in the ambient-vehicle chassis/suspension simulation, which no peer has examined
  (`M11-ki082-suspension-research-20261118.md`, part A). The earlier clip's along-motion judder
  (presentation timing) adds to it for fast cars, but it is a separate effect.
- KI-088 is not the cause of KI-082.

What would settle it with one step: a host-side probe that logs, for one nearby traffic vehicle, the
guest chassis pose that the renderer uses each frame (body world matrix y, pitch and roll), plus its
wheel contact heights and the step dt. Run it once at 30 fps (Stock) and once at about 60 fps Unlocked.
Per-frame pose noise that is present in the guest pose itself, and larger at the higher step rate,
proves it is a simulation issue. A smooth guest pose would move the search to the render path
(skinning or vertex constants). If a capture is preferred instead: hold a constant 30-40 mph directly
behind one lit car for 10 s, once Stock 30 and once Unlocked, and record at 120 fps without duplicate
filling.
