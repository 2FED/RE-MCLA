## 2. Attribution

- **The texture heaps are the stutter fix.** Every tail metric moves only in the arms that have heaps (heaps and
  all3):
  - p99 frame time 53 -> 36-38 ms;
  - frames >50 ms per minute 29 -> 0-6;
  - max frame 94-99 -> 46-63 ms;
  - in the fixed post-F10 window, 13 -> 0 frames >50 ms and max 83 -> 38 ms;
  - PresentMon 1 % low 24 -> 30 fps (an independent, host-side source).

  The timer arm alone stays at the default level: p99 47-48 ms, 23-25 frames >50 ms/min, and 7 frames >50 ms in the
  fixed window.
- **Why the heaps help.** Texture creation on the CP (GPU Commands) thread falls from 383-418 us average (one
  `CreateCommittedResource` per texture, a kernel allocation) to 16-22 us, at about 7000 creates per minute.
  - Committed path, per race minute: about 2.8 s of CP time, single creates up to 12-18 ms (43 ms in the loading
    minute). These costs bunch up when the game streams new city blocks, which is the traversal/intersection
    stutter.
  - With heaps, the only long creates are the 6-9 ms creation of a new 256 MB heap, 5 times per race.
  - The CP-side texture work drops by 1.4-1.6 ms per frame (p_tex 4.3-4.5 -> 2.7-3.0 ms/f), and CP busy drops by
    about 2 ms per frame.
  - The GPU stays fed: 84-88 -> 92-94 % busy, render_wait 7.6 -> 5.6-5.9 ms/f.

  That explains the +3 fps average with peak fps unchanged: the peaks are GPU-bound, and the CP-bound streaming
  moments got shorter.
- **The timer switch: no measurable fps or stutter effect on this PC.**
  - The spin loop costs only about 7 % of one core here (yield/sleep dominates), not the full core the design notes
    assumed. Blocking cuts that to 2-3 % of a core.
  - The CPU stays pinned at 35 W / STAPM 100 % in every arm, and the game's CPU share is unchanged (23.8 vs 24.3 %).
    The freed 0.05 core cannot visibly speed up the CP.
  - The small timer-arm gains over default (+1.5-2.7 fps, p99 -5 ms) are within one-run driving variance, and its
    texture create count was slightly lower.
  - all3 equals heaps within noise: p99 37.9/36.8 vs 36.3/37.9, >50 ms 6/0 vs 0/3.
  - The real timer gain is **tick accuracy**. On Windows the spin path's periodic 1 ms sleep oversleeps: 42 % of
    ticks are more than 1 ms late and 85 % more than 250 us late. The blocking high-resolution timer has 0.2-0.4 %
    of ticks more than 1 ms late and 25 % more than 250 us late. Max lateness is unchanged at 2.4-6 ms in the race.
- **Loading.** The default arm's loading minute had a 1.1-1.9 s `MCLA_CP_FREEZE` episode (stack in
  D3D12Core/nvwgf2umx) and a 1716 ms max frame. The other arms' loading maxima were 349-449 ms. This is consistent
  with driver allocation cost, but loading also includes pipeline-cache creation, so it is not attributed.

## 5. Recommendation for the next PC build defaults

1. **`d3d12_texture_placed_heaps` = on (256 MB blocks on PC).** It is the clear stutter win: p99 -30 %, >50 ms
   frames almost gone, +3 fps average. It had 0 fallbacks, 0 heap failures and 0 oversized textures, and VRAM was
   about 400-700 MB lower than committed at the same race time (local_mb 4328 vs 4862-5039), even though up to one
   block can sit reserved and unused.
   - Before tester defaults, check AMD/Intel drivers: run once on the RX 6800 XT (driver-specific placed-resource
     risk) and get the owner's visual check for texture corruption.
   - Optional follow-up: pre-create the first 1-2 heaps during loading, to move the 6-9 ms heap-creation hitches
     out of the race.
2. **`timer_queue_blocking_wait` = on.** It is neutral for fps/stutter on the PC. The tick is far more accurate:
   >1 ms late falls from 42 % to 0.3 %. It uses 2-3 % instead of about 7 % of a core, and there was no stall or lost
   wake.
   - The Deck runs must confirm the "high-resolution waitable timer ok" log line under Proton (no fallback to the
     spin path).
   - The Deck runs must also report `MCLA_TIMERQ` late_1ms / max_late_us under load, the TimerQueue thread CPU and
     the game CPU on 4C/8T, and any KI-060 audio underruns against deck/default.
3. **`xma_canary_fixes`: keep off until the by-ear arm passes.** It had no measurable effect and zero triggers. It
   can be enabled later as a safety net once cut-scene/radio playback is confirmed clean.
4. **Deck `heaps64`: what to confirm.**
   - `MCLA_TEXHEAP` heaps / reserved_mb vs used_mb, against the Deck's VRAM budget (`MCLA_VRAM` local_budget_mb,
     near_budget_steps).
   - With 64 MB blocks, textures above 32 MB go committed: watch `oversized` and `fallbacks`.
   - Heap creation count and heap_create_us_max: more, smaller heaps means more but shorter hitches.
   - heap_failures = 0.
   - Long hitches together with high reserved_mb: VidMm paging whole heaps.
   - The same p99 / >50 ms per minute comparison against deck/default.

## Limits

One run per arm, and the driving differs (heaps had a slow-motion event and finished 8-17 s later). Only two full
race pacing minutes exist per run. Frame-pacing percentiles are per minute, so no race-wide percentile can be
computed. The dip table is a sampled lower-bound measure. Sensor CSVs are treated as untrusted and parsed only. Spark
was not used: these are deterministic parses of four logs plus two CSVs, and a model answer would be more expensive
to check than the parse itself.
