## 1. Counters per arm

### Steady delivery (race windows, READBACK_SYNC minute/mark lines)

### CP lead indicators

No direct CP-lead counter exists (submission_current - submission_completed is not logged). Proxies:

- Steady arms (M11-007c): at draw 34 of frame N the CP polls the frame N-1 copy (draw 34 of N-1).
  `fast` = the GPU had already passed it (lead < 1 frame); `wait` = it had not (lead >= 1 frame), and
  `steady_wait_us` is how far the GPU still was from N-1 draw 34. With waits of 3-4 ms mean (max ~10 ms
  in the rings), the lead at draw 34 oscillates between ~0.8 and ~1.4 frames and is capped there by
  the bounded wait. `prev_age` (submissions between the two slots) is 5 in wait runs and 5-17 in
  fast runs.
- l75 (no bounded wait): skip episodes ran until a rule-b wait drained the queue; rule-b waits up to
  36-50 ms show the lead reached ~1.5-2 frames.
- Base (l75 b632bc55, l74 00c0cb3c/2a20bf46): `forced_c` ~= 1 per frame (2162 per 2154 frames, 2142/2120,
  2091/2081), `targeted_gpu_busy` ~= forced (the GPU was still busy at almost every wait), wait
  5.8-7.3 ms per frame, base ring: 41 of 41 frames `sync_forced=1`, `prev_age=0`, mean wait 5.8 ms. The
  CP waits at draw 26 of every frame until the GPU has finished draw 26 of the same frame: the lead is
  reset to 0 once per frame and never exceeds one frame. **Confirmed.**

## 3. Test of "CP lead + early fence"

Predictions of the theory and what the data say:

1. **More lead -> more defects.** Not supported.
   - Lead per arm: base ~0 (forced own-slot wait every frame) -> no flicker ever reported in a base run.
     l75 steady (lead unbounded until a rule-b drain, up to ~1.5-2 frames) -> 1 and 5 flicker presses.
     l76 (lead capped at ~1.0-1.4 frames, waits never time out) -> 1-3 flicker episodes per run,
     same rate as l74/l75. Halving the maximum lead did not reduce the rate.
   - Within a run, the highest-lead stretches (0-20 s, 50-80 s; 55-60 of 80 frames waited) had no flicker
     press in any of 9 steady runs. Presses cluster at +130..+175 s (mid lead) and the floating car was
     captured at +91 s in the lowest-lead stretch of the route, after 127 of 129 frames had been fast.
   - ensure vs steady: identical lead and delivery (ensure never fired on the road); ensure 2's "whole
     objects despawned" is therefore not an ensure effect but run-to-run variation of the same defect.
2. **Defect vanishes right after a forced rule-b wait drains the queue.** Not supported (weak, owner
   reaction time unknown): ensure1 +155.7 was pressed 14 frames after six rule-b drains in 45 frames;
   ensure2 +129.0 14 frames after seven drains; s2 +152.8 three frames after a 19 ms drain.
3. **Base has lead ~0**: confirmed (section 1). But base also differs in frame rate (33-36 vs 40-42 fps),
   in CP-bound vs GPU-bound pacing, and in guest RAM content of 039CA000 (own slot N vs slot N-1). Those
   three are confounded with the lead in every A/B so far.

## 4. GPU->CPU paths besides resolves

- **memexport: not used by the game.** No memexport line in any l76 log; the 12 sessions with the M10
  watch census on (driving included) all report `memexport=req:0,fast:0,up:0,...`. (SDK: memexport
  read-back is on by default, `readback_memexport_fast`: the double-buffered copy delivers the previous
  slot if complete, else a full `AwaitAllQueueOperationsCompletion`. Not exercised by MCLA.)
- **Occlusion queries: one user, harmless.** The game issues EVENT_WRITE_ZPD from one place:
  `sub_824302F0` (packet 0xC0005B00, event 21) <- `sub_8241DE08` (D3D query Issue) <- rage
  `sub_82179710` (begin) / `sub_82179718` (end), with `sub_82179720` (GetData, non-blocking, flags 0)
  used only by `sub_822DD088` (a double-buffered query around one small draw that stores a float
  visibility ratio: sun/flare-type visibility, not object culling). The SDK
  (`EndGuestOcclusionQuery`) ends the submission and polls its fence once, immediately; if the GPU has not
  finished it (practically always) it writes the fake count 1000 ("visible"). The result is written in CP
  order before the following fence, so the guest never reads it early, and the fake value can only make
  something more visible. It cannot cause despawns, and the arms do not differ.
- **CPU texture/VB locks**: the lock probe shows that no lock overlaps any read-back key during the race,
  so the game CPU never reads 039CA000 or any other resolve target on the road (section 1).
- **CPU-written data gated by the fence (VB/IB/constant/texture reuse)**: the CP copies guest memory into
  upload buffers when it parses the draw (`RequestRange`), before it parses the following fence. An early
  fence therefore cannot hand the GPU overwritten CPU data. The streaming replay worker only replays recorded
  D3D12 commands and never reads guest memory.
- **Texture cache view of 039CA000**: every `tex_inval` in all eight sessions is `by_gpu=1` (none by a CPU
  write); 039CA000 loads are `scaled_resolve=1`. The CP's memcpy of the N-1 slot into guest RAM does not
  feed back into the GPU copy.
- **Not covered by the forced waits and not logged**: the fence word itself (EVENT_WRITE_SHD at every
  segment kick, read by the render thread's ring-space/frame-throttle waits `sub_82411E98` /
  `sub_82412F98`), MEM_WRITE / interrupt-signalled values, and the swap/vblank counters
  (`guest_vblank_sequence`; vblank runs at 240 Hz during the race). The steady arms change only how far
  these run ahead of the GPU. No game consumer of GPU-produced data behind them has been found.

## 5. Conclusion

- **The early-fence mechanism has no identified consumer.** Every GPU->CPU data path the game uses on the
  road is accounted for: resolves (no CPU lock), memexport (unused), occlusion query (one flare-type user,
  fake-visible, order-correct). CPU->GPU data are captured at parse time. The data also show no dose-response:
  capping the lead at ~1 frame (l76) did not reduce the rate seen with an unbounded lead (l75), the
  highest-lead stretches never flickered, and the one captured defect (floating car) came at the
  lowest lead of the route.
- **Most likely mechanism (by elimination, not yet proven): a game-side reaction to the changed pacing,
  i.e. frame rate and GPU-bound scheduling, not stale GPU data.** Every exempt arm runs 40-42 fps with
  the guest render thread throttled by the GPU (render_wait ~6 ms), against 33-36 fps CP-bound in base
  (render_wait ~11 ms). Floating traffic cars and despawning objects are typical symptoms of
  traffic/LOD/streaming placement running at a different dt or order. The same stretches recur in every
  run because the route sets the load and speed there. Compare KI-041 (unlocked-mode flicker that only
  shows at some resolutions/frame rates) and KI-043. A second, weaker candidate is a host GPU-side hazard
  that only appears when more than one frame of work is queued (resource/slot reuse keyed on submission
  completion). It would show as a defect correlated with `w` runs, which the rings do not show.
- **Before M11-007e (deferred fence), run a cheap control that breaks the confound:** exempt arm vs base
  arm with the frame rate held equal (a fixed-step/locked timing mode or a frame cap at ~33 fps for both),
  plus an exempt arm with a per-frame CP drain at the swap (lead 0, exemption kept). Flicker with
  exempt+cap or exempt+drain refutes "fps"/"lead" respectively; flicker only uncapped points at the game's
  frame-rate sensitivity, and the deferred fence would not fix it.
- **If M11-007e is built, it must log** (minute/mark line plus the F10 ring):
  1. Per EVENT_WRITE_SHD (and MEM_WRITE/interrupt) fence value: CP parse time and submission index, GPU
     completion time of that submission, and `early_by` = frames and ms between the CP write and GPU
     completion. Histogram per minute, last 200 in the F10 ring.
  2. A host hook on the guest fence wait (`sub_82411E98` BlockOnFence and the throttle `sub_82412F98`):
     per call the fence awaited, `lr`, guest thread, whether it returned while the GPU had not completed
     the fence's submission (`early_return`), and how many frames early. Count per minute, with the top
     callers by `lr`. This names any consumer the theory needs. If all early returns are only the render
     thread's ring/throttle waits, the theory is dead.
  3. CP lead at each swap: `submission_current - submission_completed` and frames in flight (min/avg/max
     per minute), in the F10 ring per frame with a timestamp so the press can be aligned.
  4. Deferred-fence queue depth and the stall time it adds to the guest (render_wait delta), fps.
  5. Occlusion queries: begin/end count, fake vs real results per minute (to confirm the single flare-type
     user stays harmless).
  6. Per-frame timestamps in the resolve ring (currently none), so that the ~3 s before a press can be
     matched to frame intervals.
- Owner-facing: the floating car at 1:16 on the 405 Freeway (steady 1) is the first captured instance of
  the l76 defect. Despawns were not captured. The steady default stays OFF and 039CA000 stays forced.
